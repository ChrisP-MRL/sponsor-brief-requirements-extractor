SCOR Model for Demand Planning

Mining Technology Project Demand Planning Analysis — Claude Code Rule

Framework SCOR (Supply Chain Operations Reference)
Domain Mining Technology Projects
Supply Model Engineer-to-Order (ETO)
Project Horizon 3–10 years typical

Purpose

Evaluate and analyze demand requirements for mining technology projects using SCOR (Supply Chain Operations Reference) framework principles, enabling structured demand forecasting, risk assessment, and supply chain optimization for complex, geographically distributed mining operations.

Applicability

This rule applies to mining technology projects characterized by:

  • Complex, customized equipment deployments (Engineer-to-Order model)
  • Multi-year development timelines (3–10 years typical)
  • Geographic remoteness and environmental challenges
  • High capital intensity and commodity price volatility
  • Integration of emerging technologies (electrification, autonomy, digital integration)
  • Multiple stakeholder coordination (mining operators, equipment manufacturers, logistics providers)

SCOR Process Coverage

The framework maps the five SCOR processes plus Enable onto the sections of this rule.

2.1 Demand Forecasting & Production Planning

Methodology: Multi-Scenario Demand Forecasting

Develop three distinct demand scenarios reflecting commodity price and operational uncertainty inherent to mining:

ABase Case Scenario

  • Methodology: Historical commodity price trends + industry analyst forecasts
  • Assumptions:
    • Commodity price range: [Low], [Middle], [High]
    • Market share assumption: [X]%
    • Production ramp-up timeline: [Y] months to full capacity
    • Equipment utilization rate: [Z]%
  • Demand Output: Annual production volumes for years 1–10 of operation
  • Equipment Deployment Timeline: Specify equipment quantities and deployment windows

BUpside Scenario (High Demand Case)

  • Driver: Stronger-than-expected commodity prices or market demand surge
  • Assumptions:
    • Commodity price: +[X]% above base case
    • Market share: +[Y]% upside
    • Production acceleration: [Z] months earlier than base case
  • Implications: Accelerated equipment deployment, increased working capital, potential bottlenecks in supplier capacity

CDownside Scenario (Low Demand Case)

  • Driver: Weaker commodity prices or market demand contraction
  • Assumptions:
    • Commodity price: −[X]% below base case
    • Market share: −[Y]% downside
    • Production delay: [Z] months later than base case
  • Implications: Deferred equipment deployment, reduced working capital requirements, potential extended equipment storage

Forecast Accuracy Assessment

  • Historical forecasting accuracy (if project is expansion of existing operation):
    • Actual vs. forecast variance for previous projects: [X]%
    • MAPE (Mean Absolute Percentage Error) benchmark for mining sector: 15–25%

Factors degrading forecast accuracy:

  • Commodity price volatility (primary driver for mining)
  • Geographic/operational discovery (unexpected geological conditions)
  • Technology evolution (equipment specifications may require updating)
  • Market timing unpredictability
  • Regulatory changes affecting viability

11.2 Common Pitfalls & Mitigation

Pitfall 1: Treating SCOR as Checklist Rather Than Strategic Framework

Risk: Implementing processes superficially without driving real business value.

Mitigation:

  • Start with strategic imperatives (cost reduction, risk mitigation, timeline acceleration)
  • Map supply chain improvements directly to strategic objectives
  • Focus on end-to-end value chains, not individual processes in isolation
  • Measure outcomes (business results), not just process compliance

Pitfall 2: One-Size-Fits-All Process Definition

Risk: Applying same processes to all products/suppliers despite significant differences.

Mitigation:

  • Segment products/equipment by characteristics (volume, value, complexity)
  • Apply different SCOR configurations to different segments
  • Ensure level 2 and 3 processes reflect actual business model differences
  • Avoid “averaging away issues” through excessive generalization

Pitfall 3: KPI Overload & Focus Dilution

Risk: Measuring too many metrics without clear prioritization.

Mitigation:

  • Limit to 8–15 key demand planning KPIs (research shows this is optimal)
  • Ensure each KPI connects to strategic priority
  • Balance multiple attributes (reliability, responsiveness, agility, cost, asset efficiency)
  • Review quarterly to eliminate metrics that no longer drive decisions

Pitfall 4: Insufficient Data Quality

Risk: Using poor-quality data for forecasting and decision-making.

Mitigation:

  • Invest in master data governance (products, suppliers, locations)
  • Implement data validation and cleansing procedures
  • Reconcile data across systems before analysis
  • Establish data ownership and accountability

Pitfall 5: Resistance to Change & Lack of Buy-In

Risk: People continue old practices despite new processes.

Mitigation:

  • Secure executive sponsorship and visible commitment
  • Engage teams early in process design
  • Provide comprehensive training on new processes and tools
  • Create incentives aligned with SCOR metrics and improvement targets
  • Celebrate early wins to build momentum

Section 12: Conclusion & Next Steps

This comprehensive rule provides a structured framework for evaluating and analyzing demand for mining technology projects using SCOR principles. The framework addresses:

  • Project context definition — understanding mining operation characteristics, technology requirements, and market drivers
  • SCOR planning process — multi-scenario demand forecasting, capacity planning, and business rules alignment
  • Supplier evaluation — rigorous supplier selection, contract management, and performance monitoring
  • Quality & customization — Engineer-to-Order process management and acceptance criteria
  • Logistics optimization — transportation mode analysis, partner evaluation, and delivery scheduling
  • Maintenance & reverse logistics — spare parts planning and end-of-life management
  • Governance & risk management — organizational structure, KPIs, and risk mitigation
  • Emerging technology integration — AI-powered forecasting, IoT visibility, blockchain, autonomous systems
  • Continuous improvement — maturity progression, improvement roadmap, benefits realization

Implementation Success Factors

  1. Executive sponsorship and commitment of resources
  2. Cross-functional team engagement and stakeholder alignment
  3. Clear linkage between SCOR processes and strategic business objectives
  4. Investment in technology enablers (ERP, analytics, visibility platforms)
  5. Disciplined performance management with meaningful KPIs
  6. Continuous improvement culture with regular process reviews
  7. Adaptation to mining industry specifics (remoteness, customization, commodity volatility)

Next Steps

  1. Conduct project-specific assessment using this framework
  2. Facilitate stakeholder alignment workshop on SCOR strategy
  3. Baseline current performance against SCOR metrics
  4. Prioritize top 3–5 improvement initiatives
  5. Establish governance structure and decision authority
  6. Develop detailed 12-month implementation roadmap
  7. Secure budget and resource allocation
  8. Begin execution with quick wins and early momentum-building

For specialized mining technology projects, this framework enables organizations to optimize demand planning, reduce supply chain costs, improve equipment delivery reliability, and enhance overall project success through systematic application of proven SCOR principles and best practices.
